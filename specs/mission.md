# VoteFeed Mission

Last updated: 2026-05-11

## What I Am Building

I'm building VoteFeed to help people understand, track, and act on their political representation.

Right now, that work starts with the U.S. House. That is the first place I'm proving the workflow. Long term, I want VoteFeed to become a clear, trustworthy way for a constituent to follow and respond to the federal, state, and local legislators who make decisions on their behalf.

Votefeed provides a digital space for the relationship between a person and the people who represent them.

## The Core Job

VoteFeed should help a user move from lack of policy knowledge to civic action.

VoteFeed should make it easier for someone to:

1. understand who represents them
2. see what that representative actually did
3. understand the bill or vote in question
4. decide where they stand
5. respond through a real channel

If a feature does not strengthen that loop, it is probably not core VoteFeed work.

## The Throughline

The throughline for VoteFeed is simple:

people should not have to guess what their representatives did, and they should not have to dig through a maze just to respond.

I want the product to make representation feel:

- legible
- local
- grounded in real votes
- actionable

## Scope Now, Scope Later

### Current Build Focus

The current implementation is focused on the U.S. House because it gives the clearest place to prove the experience end to end:

- resolve the district
- identify the representative
- pull the vote record
- show the bill context
- help the user respond

### Long-Term Product Direction

The long-term product is broader than the current build.

My plan is for VoteFeed to eventually support the overall representation relationship between a constituent and:

- federal legislators
- state legislators
- local legislators

That expansion should happen carefully. I do not want to pile on new chambers before the core experience feels trustworthy and sharp in the first one.

## Product Principles

### Start Where The Person Lives

VoteFeed should begin with the user's actual place in government, not with abstract political content. District and locality are not side details here. They are the basis of the relationship.

### Stay Anchored To What Actually Happened

VoteFeed should be rooted in real legislative behavior. Real votes. Real bills. Real representatives.

### Make Action Real, Not Performative

The product should help someone do something real with what they learned. That can mean understanding a vote, forming a stance, calling an office, or using an official contact form. It should not mean giving people the illusion of action without a real path behind it.

### Use Official Sources Whenever It Matters

Representative identity, district mapping, vote records, and office contact paths should come from official or otherwise trusted civic sources. If something is missing or stale, the product should say so plainly instead of pretending otherwise.

## Current Product Direction

The current product still carries some older social features, especially around approval, disapproval, and comments. I do not see that as the deepest long-term center of the product.

The stronger direction is this:

- help the user understand the vote
- help the user take a position
- help the user respond through a real, official path

That is a stronger mission than building another place for political commentary.

## Copy And Trust Rules

- The product should say clearly what VoteFeed does.
- The product should say clearly what VoteFeed does not do.
- If VoteFeed prepares a script, draft, or action panel, the copy should not imply that VoteFeed sent anything on the user's behalf.
- If a contact path is incomplete, unavailable, or uncertain, the product should say that directly.

## Decision Filter

When I decide whether something belongs in VoteFeed, I want to ask:

Does this make the relationship between a constituent and their representatives clearer, more trustworthy, and more actionable?

If yes, it probably belongs.

If it mainly makes the product noisier, more generic, or more performative, it probably does not.
